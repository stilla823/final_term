Between the Lines

Type a short dialogue scene. Get a three-shot storyboard where the camera angle, position and framing are chosen to show what the scene means.

Design Spec, version 1. Final Term Project, due 16 Nov 2026. Your Name, Student ID

The saved demo result for the sample scene. Panels are drawn in code (hand-drawn look by Rough.js) from original composition templates and a camera angle for each shot.

1The idea

Between the Lines helps film students and beginner filmmakers turn a short dialogue scene into a three-shot storyboard. The user types the dialogue for one or two characters and can choose whether the scene is indoor or outdoor and add what the audience should feel or understand. The app returns three storyboard panels, each with its shot size, camera angle, camera position, character placement, visible action and one acting note, plus one optional action or line to add before the scene and one after, kept apart from the original script. It matters because beginners often know what a scene says but not how to show it. Seeing the scene broken into three concrete shots, each with a reason tied to the directing intention, gives them something specific to discuss, test and change, whether they are planning a new scene or looking for a different way to direct one they have already written.

The app is built around how a film camera works. Where the camera stands changes what the audience feels about a character: a low angle looks up and makes the character look large and in control, an eye-level shot keeps characters equal, and a high angle looks down and makes the character look small and anxious. Each shot gets one of these angles, with a reason tied to the directing intention.

How the camera angle changes a shot

Every shot has two separate choices. The composition template says what is in the frame. The camera angle says where the camera stands. The app draws the panel from both, so a change of angle is visible in the picture: a low angle shows a ceiling and larger figures, a high angle shows a wide floor and smaller figures.

AngleWhat the camera doesUsual effect on the audienceIn the sample scene
Low angleSits below the character and looks upThe character looks large and in control: power, confidence, menaceNot used. It would make B look strong, which works against the intention.
Eye levelSits at the character's eye heightNeutral. The characters look equalShot 1, where A and B are still on equal terms
High angleSits above the character and looks downThe character looks small, vulnerable, anxious, isolatedShots 2 and 3, both about B

In the sample, the directing intention is "B wants to go but cannot hold on", so the app picks the high angle for B: B looks small and anxious. A low angle would make B look strong and in control, which works against the intention.

These effects are long-standing conventions, not rules. The meaning of an angle depends on the story, the performance and the shots around it, and the app tells the user so. The text that explains each angle comes from a catalog in the repository, so the model only chooses an angle and explains why; it cannot change what the angle means.

  1. Mode labelAlways visible. Says whether the app is in demo mode or live AI mode.
  2. Character countChoose 1 or 2. Decides which speakers are allowed (A only, or A and B).
  3. Dialogue fieldOne line per turn, written A: 대사 or B: 대사. Checked before anything is generated.
  4. LocationA select box with two choices: indoor (실내) or outdoor (실외). If the model assumes a more specific place, it states the assumption.
  5. Directing intention (optional)What the audience should feel or understand. The reason on each shot refers back to it.
  6. Load sampleFills the whole form with the sample scene.
  7. Make storyboardChecks the dialogue, then shows the saved result (demo) or generates one (live).
  8. Shot card, three timesPanel, shot size, camera angle, camera position, placement and action, dialogue, acting note, reason.
  9. AI 연출 제안One optional suggestion before and one after the scene, each with what it changes. Never merged into the script or the shots.

4User flow

  1. The user opens the app. It starts in demo mode.
  2. The user presses 예시 불러오기, or chooses 1 or 2 characters and indoor or outdoor, and types a scene.
  3. The user presses 스토리보드 만들기.
  4. The app checks the dialogue. If a line is wrong, it names the line and the rule, and the user fixes it and presses the button again.
  5. The app shows three shot cards, each with its camera angle, and below them AI 연출 제안.
  6. The user edits the scene and repeats from step 3.

Error and edge cases

The required error case is the speaker outside the cast. The others are the cases most likely to confuse a user, so each one has a message that says what happened and what to do next.

Speaker outside the cast (main error case)

Input: 2 characters selected, line 2 is “C: 안녕”.

2번째 줄의 화자 C는 선택한 인원에 없습니다. 2명 장면에서는 A와 B만 쓸 수 있어요.

Nothing is generated. The user fixes the line or changes the character count.

Empty dialogue, or a line without a speaker label

Input: empty field, or line 3 reads “다음에 보자”.

대사를 입력해 주세요. 한 줄에 한 마디씩 “A: 대사” 형식으로 적습니다.
3번째 줄에 화자 표시가 없습니다. “A: 대사” 또는 “B: 대사” 형식으로 써 주세요.

6AI and prompts

ModeModelHow it is set up
Demo (default)NoneShows the saved result for the unchanged sample scene. No key, download or paid service.
LiveA locally running open-source instruction model, not yet chosenBehind a separate adapter (model_adapter.py). The endpoint and model name are read from settings outside the source code, for example LLM_ENDPOINT and LLM_MODEL.

How the model will be chosen. The license allows free use, it runs on an ordinary laptop, it follows a JSON format reliably, and it writes acceptable Korean. The choice and its license will be written into this page and the README. The adapter can also be pointed at another model, including Claude, without changing the rest of the app.

Key prompt 1: instructions (system message)

Turn the input scene into exactly three filmable shots. Use only the characters
provided. Preserve the original dialogue and its order by referencing the
supplied dialogue line IDs. Choose a composition template from the supplied
catalog for each shot. Choose a camera angle from the supplied catalog for each
shot and say which character the angle is about. For every shot, return the
template ID, character placement, camera position, visible action, acting note,
and a brief reason for the directing choice. Ground the choices in the directing
intention when one is provided. If you assume a location or an emotional
interpretation, state the assumption. Return optional before-scene and after-
scene suggestions in separate fields. Return structured JSON.

The same message adds a short camera angle guide, a note that the location is only "indoor" or "outdoor", and the JSON shape. The guide says these are conventions and the angle must be chosen for a reason that fits the directing intention:

Camera angle guide (conventions, not rules; pick the angle for a reason that fits the directing intention):
- low: the camera looks up. The character looks larger, powerful, in control.
- eye_level: neutral. The characters look equal.
- high: the camera looks down. The character looks smaller, vulnerable, anxious, isolated.

Check before anything is shown

  • There are exactly three shots.
  • Every template ID and angle ID is in its catalog. Every character, including the one an angle is about, is one the user provided, and the roles fit the template.
  • The dialogue line IDs, read across the three shots, include every original line exactly once and in the original order.
  • The dialogue text on screen comes from the user's input, looked up by ID. The model never supplies it, so it cannot rewrite it.
  • What an angle means (the effect shown on the card) comes from the app's catalog. The model chooses the angle and gives the reason.
  • If any check fails, the app says the storyboard could not be generated and offers to retry or load the demo.

7System structure

Four parts, built only from free and open tools. Demo mode uses the first two layers and the renderer. Live mode adds the dashed box.

Interface: app.py (Streamlit)

Input form, mode label, dialogue check, error messages, three shot cards and the suggestions section.

↓

Demo mode: data/demo.json

The saved sample scene and its saved result. Shown only while the input matches the sample.

Live mode: model_adapter.py

Builds the prompt, calls a local open-source model, parses the JSON and runs the checks from section 6. Scaffolded first, so live mode is the part that may remain planned.

↓

Renderer: storyboard.py, data/templates.json, data/camera_grammar.json

Draws each panel as SVG from the chosen template and camera angle, plus a small side view of the camera. The same code serves demo and live results.

8Risks and limits

What could go wrongHow the design responds
The model rewrites, reorders or drops dialogueIt returns line IDs only. The app prints the user's own text by ID and checks that every line is used once, in order.
The reply is malformed: wrong shot count, unknown template or characterThe reply is validated. On failure the user sees a clear message and can retry or load the demo.
The saved demo is mistaken for a real resultIt appears only for the unchanged sample, labeled 데모 모드 · 저장된 예시 결과. An edited scene gets a message instead.
The model reads too much into the emotionAssumptions are stated, each reason is tied to the directing intention, and the suggestions are optional and separate from the script.
A small local model gives generic or awkward KoreanKorean quality is a selection criterion. The user can retry, and the demo always works.
Live mode is not available on the user's computerA clear message and a button to load the sample demo.
The user reads an angle as a fixed rule ("high angle always means weak")The app calls angle meanings conventions, shows the other angles for the same shot, and gives the reason for the choice in the user's own scene.
The model picks an angle that contradicts the intentionThe reason must refer to the directing intention, the angle ID is validated, and the angle comparison lets the user see and judge the alternatives. The user decides.
The schematic panel is mistaken for the final imagePanels are labeled as drawn schematics. They show framing, height and placement only, not lighting, lens or acting.
The location choice is coarse, so the model guesses the wrong placeThe model must state any specific place it assumes (the saved demo says "a hallway with a door"), and the user can read it under the cards.
A user's unpublished scene is exposedNothing is stored. In live mode the text goes only to a model on the same computer.

What the app will not do

  • Write, change or "improve" the user's dialogue.
  • Handle more than two characters, or produce a number of shots other than three.
  • Produce realistic images. Panels are schematic sketches that show framing, placement, gaze and simple poses only.
  • Claim that its choices are the correct way to direct the scene. They are one option to react to.
  • Cover lens choice, camera movement, lighting or Dutch angles in this version. Only camera angle, shot size and framing are modeled.
  • Insert its suggestions into the script or the storyboard automatically.
  • Save scenes or require an account.
